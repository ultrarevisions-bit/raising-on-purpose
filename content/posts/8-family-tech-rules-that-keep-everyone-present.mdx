Your six-month-old doesn't know what a phone is, but she already knows when your eyes aren't on her. Babies track faces. They notice when yours is tilted down at a screen instead of up, watching them pull themselves up on the coffee table for the first time. That's the weird thing about family tech rules at the baby stage: your kid can't ask you to put the phone away, so you have to build the boundary before they're old enough to need it in words.

I'm not anti-phone. I've used mine to survive a 3am feeding by watching trash TV with one hand. But there's a difference between using your phone and disappearing into it while your baby is awake and reaching for you. These eight rules aren't about perfection. They're about catching yourself more often than not.

## Phones Stay Out of the First Twenty Minutes After Waking

However your baby wakes you up, whether it's a 5:45am cry through the monitor or a 7am babble from the crib, try to let those first minutes be screen-free. No checking texts, no scrolling the news.

This isn't about some magical morning routine. It's about your baby's first interaction of the day being your actual face, not the top of your head while you check your phone.

## No Phones During Feeding Time, Bottle or Breast

Feeding is one of the few times a day your baby is held still, close, and looking up at you. At four months old, babies start making real eye contact during feeds, sometimes pulling off just to grin at you.

If you miss that because you're scrolling, you won't get it back. Set the phone in another room during feeds if you have to. It's one of the easiest family tech rules to actually keep, because your hands are usually full anyway.

## Nobody Films More Than They Watch

First steps, first laugh, first time eating spaghetti and wearing most of it. It's tempting to capture everything. But if you're watching your baby's first laugh through a phone screen instead of your own eyes, you missed it live.

Pick your moments. Film some, watch the rest. Your kid will have plenty of video evidence of childhood without you narrating every single second from behind a lens.

## One Phone-Free Meal a Day, Even If It's Just You and the Baby

This one sounds silly when your baby is eight months old and eating pureed sweet potato off a tray. But the habit you build now is the one that sticks when they're four and narrating their entire day at dinner.

Pick one meal, breakfast, lunch, doesn't matter which. Phones in another room, just you and the kid and the mess.

## Partners Get a Heads Up Before Diving Into Work Email

If you're a two parent household, this one saves actual arguments. Instead of one parent silently checking out into their phone while the other handles bath time solo, just say it out loud.

"I need ten minutes for this email, can you take her?" takes five seconds and prevents the slow burn resentment that builds when someone feels like they're parenting alone next to a person on their phone.

## Bedtime Routine Is a No Phone Zone, Full Stop

The fifteen or twenty minutes before your baby falls asleep matter more than almost any other part of the day. It's quiet, it's close, and it's often the calmest your baby will be.

Put the phone in a different room during bath, pajamas, books, and the final rocking or nursing session. Your baby doesn't need background scrolling. They need your undivided, slightly boring presence.

## Social Media Posting Happens Later, Not in the Moment

Your baby smiles for the first time at 2:15 on a Tuesday afternoon. You do not need to post about it at 2:16. Save the caption for nap time.

This rule protects you more than anyone. The urge to document and share instantly pulls you out of the moment and into performance mode, thinking about captions instead of just grinning back at your kid.

## Set a Visible Charging Spot That Isn't the Bedroom or Living Room

This is the quiet, structural rule that makes all the others easier. If your phone charges in the kitchen overnight instead of on your nightstand, you're less likely to reach for it during a 2am feed or the first groggy minutes of morning.

Out of arm's reach means out of habit. It's a small shift, but it does more heavy lifting than any willpower based rule ever will.

## These Rules Aren't About Guilt

You will mess this up. You'll scroll during a feed because you're exhausted and the baby monitor app counts as a phone use too, technically. That's fine.

The point of family tech rules isn't a flawless record. It's building habits early, while your baby is too young to notice the exceptions, so that by the time they're old enough to notice, presence is just how your family operates.

Start with one rule this week. Maybe it's the phone-free feed, maybe it's the charging spot in the kitchen. Pick the one that feels doable, not the one that feels impressive, and build from there.
