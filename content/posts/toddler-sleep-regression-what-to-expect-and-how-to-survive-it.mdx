## Your Toddler Was Sleeping Fine, So What Happened

Three weeks ago your 20-month-old went down at 7pm and didn't make a sound until morning. Now you're standing in a dark hallway at 2am for the third night in a row, wondering if you broke something. You didn't. This is a toddler sleep regression, and it's one of the most disorienting parts of the toddler years because it shows up right when you thought you were done with night wakings for good.

Regressions tend to cluster around specific ages: 18 months, 2 years, and sometimes again closer to 3. They're tied to real developmental jumps, like language explosion, molars coming in, or a toddler suddenly understanding that if they cry, you come back. Knowing that helps. It doesn't fix the exhaustion, but it means you're not doing anything wrong.

## What a Toddler Sleep Regression Actually Looks Like

It's rarely just one thing. You might get a toddler who fights bedtime for the first time ever, screaming when you leave the room. Or one who falls asleep fine but wakes at 1am and stays up for an hour, wide-eyed and chatty. Some toddlers start waking at 5am and refuse to go back down, which somehow feels worse than the middle-of-the-night wakings.

Naps often take a hit too. A kid who napped reliably at daycare might suddenly refuse to lie down, or wake up from naps cranky and fighting everyone. None of this means your toddler is broken or that you need a new routine from scratch. It usually means something's shifting underneath the surface.

## The Real Reasons Behind the Wake-Ups

A few things tend to be driving it, often at the same time.

- **Separation anxiety.** Around 18 months and again near 2, toddlers get a stronger sense of object permanence. They realize you exist even when you're not in the room, which means they also realize they can call you back.
- **Molars.** The two-year molars are brutal and can disrupt sleep for weeks, not days. If your toddler is chewing on everything and drooling more than usual, this might be it.
- **New skills.** Learning to climb out of a crib, talk in full sentences, or run instead of toddle takes up enormous mental energy. Brains practice new skills during sleep, which sometimes means less actual sleeping.
- **Testing limits.** Around 2 and especially heading into 3, toddlers start testing whether the bedtime rules are real. One extra song, one more cup of water, one more call for you. It's not manipulation, it's just a toddler figuring out where the edges are.

## How Long It Actually Lasts

Here's the part nobody wants to hear: there's no fixed timeline. Some regressions clear up in a week and a half. Others drag on for six weeks, especially if teething and a developmental leap overlap.

What I can tell you is that it does end. The toddlers who were up every two hours at 19 months are, almost without exception, sleeping fine by preschool. This is a phase, not a new permanent normal, even on night four when it feels like it might be.

## What Actually Helps During the Regression

Keep the bedtime routine the same, even when everything else feels chaotic. Bath, book, song, lights out, in that order, every single night. Predictability is the thing toddlers cling to when their world feels unstable, and a steady routine does more than any sleep gadget you'll find online.

Don't start any new habits you're not willing to keep for months. Rocking a 2-year-old back to sleep every night feels fine in the moment, but you're also teaching them that's how sleep happens, and you'll be the one undoing it later. A short, boring response works better: a hand on the back, a quiet 'I love you, time for sleep,' then leaving again.

If separation anxiety is the main driver, a small transitional object helps more than people expect. A specific stuffed animal, a worn t-shirt of yours, something that stays in the crib or bed every night as a stand-in for you. My daughter went through this at 22 months and a five dollar stuffed fox fixed more than any sleep training method we tried.

Watch total daily sleep, not just nighttime hours. A toddler who's waking at 4am might need an earlier bedtime, not a later one. Overtired toddlers sleep worse, not better, which feels backwards but is almost always true.

And lower the bar for yourself during this stretch. If you need to sit in the hallway for a few nights, do it. You can always walk it back once the regression passes. Surviving the month matters more than sticking to a method.

## When It's Worth Mentioning to Your Pediatrician

Most regressions are developmental and resolve on their own. But if the disrupted sleep lasts longer than six to eight weeks, if your toddler seems to be in pain rather than just unsettled, or if snoring and loud breathing show up alongside the wake-ups, it's worth a conversation with your pediatrician. Sleep apnea and enlarged tonsils are more common in toddlers than most parents realize, and they're worth ruling out if things aren't improving.

If you're running on fumes and nothing here feels like it's working, that's not a sign you're failing. Some stretches of toddlerhood are just genuinely hard on everyone's sleep, yours included.

This phase is loud and exhausting, but it's temporary. Hang on to the routine, keep your response boring and consistent, and trust that your toddler will find their way back to sleep. They always do.
